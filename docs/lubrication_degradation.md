# SIH26054: Lubrication Degradation Physics & Validation (Phase 4C)

## 1. Executive Summary & Objective

Phase 4C implements a physically grounded lubrication-system degradation fault within the reduced-order grey-box aero piston engine simulator of the **SIH26054 Digital Twin System**.

> ### ⚠️ Engineering Reference & Scope Disclaimer:
> This lubrication degradation model is a **reduced-order engineering fault model** designed for synthetic telemetry generation, digital twin residual tracking, and PHM diagnostic algorithm training. It is **NOT** a certified OEM failure model, classified MALE UAV flight-test recording, or CFD/EHD hydrodynamic bearing solver.

## 2. Physical Mechanism & Causal Chain

Rather than applying arbitrary offsets to output telemetry, lubrication degradation modifies the underlying hydraulic and thermal heat-balance equations:

```text
Lubrication System Degradation (e.g. oil pump wear, gallery restriction, relief valve leakage)
                                  ↓
      Hydraulic Oil Pressure Generation Degradation (P_oil ↓)
                                  ↓
       Reduced Bearing Film Thickness / Boundary Shearing Losses
                                  ↓
   Increased Frictional Heat Generation (Q_fric ↑) + Cooler Flow Restriction (h_oil_cool ↓)
                                  ↓
             Net Heat Imbalance & Rising Oil Temperature (T_oil ↑)
                                  ↓
       Viscosity Loss Amplifying Hydraulic Pressure Reduction (Compound Effect)
```

---

## 3. Governing Equations

### 3.1 Hydraulic Oil Pressure Degradation
In nominal operation, oil pressure $P_{\text{oil, nominal}}$ is generated by positive displacement camshaft pump speed and modified by fluid viscosity temperature loss:
$$P_{\text{oil, nominal}} = P_{\text{base}} + k_{\text{oil\_p\_rpm}} \cdot \text{RPM} - k_{\text{oil\_p\_temp}} \cdot \max(0, T_{\text{oil}} - 50^\circ\text{C})$$

Under lubrication degradation with effective fault severity $\sigma \in [0.0, 1.0]$:
$$P_{\text{oil, degraded}} = P_{\text{oil, nominal}} \cdot \left(1.0 - k_{\text{lub\_p\_loss}} \cdot \sigma\right)$$
$$P_{\text{oil, final}} = \max\left(0.5, \; \min\left(P_{\text{max\_relief}}, \; P_{\text{oil, degraded}}\right)\right)$$

Where $k_{\text{lub\_p\_loss}} = 0.55$ (**Tier C/D Calibration Parameter**), representing a maximum 55% loss of hydraulic delivery pressure at $\sigma = 1.0$.

### 3.2 Oil Thermal Frictional Heat Generation
Degraded lubrication thins boundary lubricant films at crankshaft journals and cylinder walls, increasing frictional dissipation transferred into the circulating oil:
$$Q_{\text{oil\_gen}} = Q_{\text{oil\_gen, base}} \cdot \left(1.0 + k_{\text{lub\_heat\_gain}} \cdot \sigma\right)$$

Where $k_{\text{lub\_heat\_gain}} = 0.20$ (**Tier C/D Calibration Parameter**), representing a 20% increase in frictional heat dissipation into the oil charge at $\sigma = 1.0$.

### 3.3 Oil Cooler Conductance Reduction
Degraded oil pump scavenging or internal circuit flow bypass reduces mass flow through the external oil radiator:
$$h_{\text{oil\_cool\_effective}} = \max\left(1.0, \; h_{\text{oil\_cool}} \cdot \left(1.0 - k_{\text{lub\_cool\_loss}} \cdot \sigma\right)\right)$$

Where $k_{\text{lub\_cool\_loss}} = 0.20$ (**Tier C/D Calibration Parameter**).

### 3.4 Compound Oil Thermal Equilibrium
$$\text{Total Conductance: } K_{\text{tot}} = h_{\text{oil\_cool\_effective}} + k_{\text{oil\_cht\_couple}}$$
$$T_{\text{oil, ss}} = \frac{Q_{\text{oil\_gen}} + h_{\text{oil\_cool\_effective}} \cdot T_{\text{ambient}} + k_{\text{oil\_cht\_couple}} \cdot T_{\text{cht}}}{K_{\text{tot}}}$$
$$C_{\text{oil}} \frac{dT_{\text{oil}}}{dt} = Q_{\text{oil\_gen}} - h_{\text{oil\_cool\_effective}} \cdot (T_{\text{oil}} - T_{\text{ambient}}) + k_{\text{oil\_cht\_couple}} \cdot (T_{\text{cht}} - T_{\text{oil}})$$

## 5. Severity Sweep & Steady-State Response

Under controlled cruise flight conditions (75% throttle, 2000 m altitude, 45 m/s airspeed, $T_{\text{ambient}} = 2.0^\circ\text{C}$):

| Fault Severity ($\sigma$) | Steady-State Oil Pressure (bar) | Steady-State Oil Temp ($^\circ\text{C}$) | CHT ($^\circ\text{C}$) | Operational Status |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **0.00** (Healthy) | **4.39 bar** | **87.5 °C** | **66.5 °C** | Nominal |
| **0.25** (Mild) | **3.74 bar** | **94.7 °C** | **66.8 °C** | Minor degradation |
| **0.50** (Moderate) | **3.10 bar** | **102.3 °C** | **67.1 °C** | Noticeable pressure drop & thermal rise |
| **0.75** (Severe) | **2.47 bar** | **110.5 °C** | **67.4 °C** | Approaching low-pressure warning threshold |
| **1.00** (Critical) | **1.86 bar** | **119.3 °C** | **67.8 °C** | Severe low oil pressure warning & elevated temp |

### Key Physical Validations:
- **Oil Pressure**: Strictly monotonically decreases from 4.39 bar down to 1.86 bar.
- **Oil Temperature**: Strictly monotonically increases from 87.5 °C up to 119.3 °C.
- **Cross-Channel Isolation**: RPM, EGT, and Fuel Flow remain uncorrupted; CHT experiences only tiny natural conduction warming from the hot oil.

## 6. Fault Scheduling, Gating & Natural Recovery

The lubrication degradation model respects the standard `FaultState` timeline:
- **Gating**: Active only when $\text{fault\_type} == \text{LUBRICATION\_DEGRADATION}$, $\text{active} == \text{True}$, $\text{severity} > 0$, and $\text{start\_time} \le t \le \text{end\_time}$.
- **Natural Recovery**: Once the fault window expires ($t > \text{end\_time}$), the hydraulic pressure immediately recovers toward nominal, and the oil temperature naturally cools down according to the oil lumped capacitance thermal dynamics ($\tau_{\text{oil}} \approx 120\text{ s}$). No artificial state resets are used.
